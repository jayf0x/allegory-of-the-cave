# Allegory of the Cave

> *Prisoners in Plato's cave mistake shadows on the wall for reality. The shadows are all they know.*

This project asks: what if an AI only ever perceives shadows?

## The idea

A camera watches a scene. What reaches the AI is not the scene itself — it's a projection, a shadow cast on the wall. The AI interprets that shadow and builds a model of the world from it.

The allegory holds: the AI, like the prisoner, can reason intelligently about a world it has never directly seen. It works from representations, not from truth.

The long-term goal is to give that prisoner **memory** — a world model that accumulates context over time, learns object permanence, infers what's behind the projection. To close the gap between shadow and reality, not by removing the wall, but by reasoning past it.

## Why it matters

Most AI perception pipelines treat each frame as isolated. There's no continuity, no model of *what exists* versus *what is currently visible*. A world model changes that. It's the difference between an AI that reacts to shadows and one that understands what's casting them.
